How to play
  1. Tap the game to start. The waiter runs by himself, with the tray on his head, along a winding path.
  2. Hold the left or right side (or press ← →) to steer. In the real Android app you tilt the phone instead.
  3. Run over the yellow circle next to a chef to catch the food they throw. Floating food and coins can be grabbed too.
  4. Dodge boxes, buckets and wet floors. Turns and sharp steering push the stack sideways, so steer smoothly.
  5. Three things on the floor and the round ends.

Which technology for a 3D version

For a 3D game the choice matters more than for 2D. Cheap phones have weak graphics chips, so the engine and its ready-made assets make a real difference.

Unity Best for 3D

Graphics
This look and better, with the URP renderer. You can buy ready-made low-poly restaurant and character packs in the Asset Store.
Cheap phones
Very good with baked lighting and simple shaders.
App size
About 40 to 70 MB
Cost
Free until you earn $200,000 a year
Watch out
The editor needs a stronger computer. C# takes longer to learn.

Best choice for a good-looking 3D game that runs on cheap phones.

Godot 4

Graphics
Can make this look. Fewer ready-made 3D packs, so you make more models yourself in Blender.
Cheap phones
Fine with the Mobile renderer, but weaker than Unity in 3D on very cheap phones.
App size
About 30 to 50 MB
Cost
Free forever, no fees
Watch out
Test on a cheap phone early. Video sharing needs a plugin.

Good if you want free and simple, and will make your own models.

A trick this demo uses

The world is 3D, but the food on the tray only moves left, right, up and down. That makes the physics 2D, which keeps tall stacks stable and is cheap for the phone. In Unity, freeze the Z position and the X and Y rotation of each food item to get the same result.
